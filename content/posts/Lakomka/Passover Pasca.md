
Pasca is a **rich, slightly sweet bread** traditionally baked for **Easter and Passover celebrations** in many **Eastern European countries**, including **Romania, Ukraine, and Moldova**. This special bread is known for its **soft, airy texture and delicate sweetness**, often enriched with **eggs, butter, and a hint of vanilla or citrus zest**.  
## **The Meaning of Pasca**  

Pasca is more than just a festive bread—it holds deep **cultural and religious significance**. In many traditions, it symbolizes **renewal, abundance, and the joy of celebration**. Families gather to bake pasca together, often preparing multiple loaves to share with **friends, neighbours, and loved ones**.  

## **What Makes Passover Pasca Special?**  

Passover pasca is made with **carefully selected ingredients** to follow **Passover dietary traditions**, ensuring it remains **fluffy, flavourful, and true to its origins**. Some variations include:  
- **Classic pasca** – A lightly sweetened bread, sometimes filled with **sweet cheese or raisins**.  
- **Cheese pasca** – A rich, custard-like version made with **farmer’s cheese, eggs, and vanilla**.  
- **Braided or round loaves** – Often decorated with a **simple cross or intricate patterns**, symbolizing tradition and faith.  

## **Enjoying Pasca During Passover**  

Pasca is best enjoyed **fresh and warm**, served with **butter, honey, or a cup of tea**. It is often part of the **holiday table**, alongside other traditional dishes, bringing **comfort and a sense of togetherness**.
